Model compare — held-out test month

Protocol: train on the first –, validate on the next month (early stopping), test on the final – with a fresh forecast origin every day (– origins). Skill is measured against two naive baselines: repeating yesterday's load (daily persistence) and repeating the same day last week (weekly persistence, MAE 2.75 MW — the honest baseline for this load, whose lag-168 autocorrelation is 0.99). 0 means no better than that baseline; negative means worse. The Transformer and PatchTST are PyTorch models trained for this twin; Random Forest and Ridge use lag and rolling-window features, in the pattern of the HydroTwin Energy roster.

Model architectures

What each model in the table above actually is — input, structure, output, and how it was trained. The three neural models share one training recipe (AdamW, learning rate 1e-3 with cosine decay, MSE loss, batch 128, at most 30 epochs, early stopping on validation MAE), so the comparison is between architectures, not tuning effort.

ModelArchitecture
TransformerEncoder-only Transformer (PyTorch). The 168 h × 6-channel window (IT load and cooling, standardized, plus hour-of-day and day-of-week sin/cos) is projected to dmodel 96 with learned positional embeddings; 2 encoder layers, 4 attention heads, feedforward 192, dropout 0.1. The time-mean of the encoded states feeds a linear head that emits all 24 h × 2 targets in one shot.
PatchTSTChannel-independent patch Transformer. Each load channel is cut into 20 patches of 16 h (stride 8); patches are embedded to dmodel 64 with learned positions and passed through a shared 2-layer, 4-head encoder (feedforward 128, dropout 0.1); the flattened patch states feed a per-channel linear head (24 h).
LSTM2-layer LSTM, hidden size 96, dropout 0.1, over the same 6-channel window; the final hidden state feeds a linear head (24 h × 2 targets).
Random Forest110 lag and rolling features per forecast origin — for each channel: yesterday's 24 hourly values, the same 24 hours last week, the 24 h and 168 h means, the last value and the value 24 h ago — plus 4 calendar sin/cos terms. One multi-output random forest per target: 200 trees, min_samples_leaf 2.
RidgeThe same 110 lag features, standardized; ridge regression with α = 10, direct 24-output prediction per target.
PersistenceBaseline with no training: repeats the previous day's 24 hours.
Persistence (weekly)Baseline with no training: repeats the same day last week (lag 168 h). The load's lag-168 autocorrelation is 0.99, so this is the baseline the learned models must beat.

What the test month costs on the grid

If the test month's electric load were bought from ERCOT at LZ_SOUTH hourly prices, this is the bill — the reference case the geothermal designs on the main page are compared against. The electric load is the IT load plus the cooling service converted as in the study LP — cooling × (1/COP + 0.0297), the second term being the chiller auxiliaries — with COP – (ASHRAE 90.1-2019, 0.560 kW/ton, from the study's parameter register). Energy only — no fees, no 4CP charge. In the cascade designs, geothermal heat drives absorption chillers instead, so most of the cooling-electric part never becomes a grid purchase.
