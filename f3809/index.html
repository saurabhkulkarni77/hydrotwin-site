F-3809 Geothermal Digital Twin

Hourly optimization of geothermal power, absorption cooling and reservoir thermal energy storage (RTES) for a 200 MWe data center at the F-3809 well field, Hidalgo County, Texas. The twin couples the study's 8,760-hour linear program with its machine-learning temperature model, extended with conformal calibration and a deep learning model.

The system

Brine is produced, run through a binary plant, then cascaded into cooling and storage. Grid import and gas CHP are the backup options the optimizer can choose instead.

Geothermal field24 producers, Vicksburg formation, brine reinjected at 70 to 93 °C
→
Binary (ORC) plantOutlet temperature chosen every hour, 70 to 160 °C, trading power against heat
→
Absorption chillersSingle-effect LiBr, COP 0.70, driven by the outlet heat
→
RTESChilled-water storage in an aquifer, ΔT 13.5 K, charged and discharged hourly
→
Data center200 MWe, direct-to-chip cooling, served every hour of the year

Backup: ERCOT grid import (LZ_SOUTH) and optional gas CHP. Every relation in the optimizer is linear, so the optimum is global and the dual prices are exact sensitivities.

Machine learning and deep learning — the temperature model

Machine learning predicts one physical parameter only: corrected bottom-hole temperature from latitude, longitude and depth, on – Texas BEG wells. Its P10–P90 interval sets the production-temperature range for the Monte Carlo; the LP itself is never replaced by a model. Validation: 5-fold cross-validation on 0.5° spatial blocks, plus a hold-out with the whole F-3809 area withheld (train beyond 25 km, test on the – wells within 5 km, measured mean – °C).
